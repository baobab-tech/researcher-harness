# Nature Separates Reviews, Perspectives, Comments, and Analyses by Voice and Whether New Analysis Is Included

**Topic:** Journal article types for non-primary outputs
**Source:** Nature (Springer Nature), author guidance pages, accessed 2026
**Type:** organisational disclosure (publisher author guidelines)
**URL:** https://www.nature.com/nature/for-authors/other-subs
**Published:** undated web page, retrieved 2026-09
**Status:** editorial policy

## Finding

Nature publishes Reviews, which focus on one topical aspect of a field and indicate opposing views
where controversial, and Perspectives, which let authors "discuss models and ideas from a personal
viewpoint," are "more forward looking and/or speculative," and "may be opinionated but should remain
balanced." Both are peer-reviewed. Comment pieces are commissioned, "agenda-setting" expert pieces
that "road-map a proposed solution"; they do not only describe a problem. Analysis articles report
no original data but present a new analysis of existing data. Correspondence and Matters Arising
handle responses to published material. Nature Reviews Psychology's content page adds World View
(a call to action from personal experience) and Research Highlights (editor summaries of one or two
papers).

## Key Data

| Type | Peer-reviewed | Voice | New analysis |
|------|---------------|-------|--------------|
| Review | yes | balanced survey of one topic | no |
| Perspective | yes | personal, forward-looking | no |
| Comment | commissioned | advocacy with a proposed solution | no |
| Analysis | yes | review-based | yes, of existing data |
