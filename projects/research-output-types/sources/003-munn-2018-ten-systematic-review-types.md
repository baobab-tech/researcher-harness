# Ten Types of Systematic Review, Distinguished by the Question Asked

**Topic:** Subtypes of systematic review and their question frameworks
**Source:** Zachary Munn, Cindy Stern, Edoardo Aromataris, Craig Lockwood, Zoe Jordan (JBI, University of Adelaide), 2018
**Type:** peer-reviewed
**URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5761190/
**DOI:** 10.1186/s12874-017-0468-4
**Published:** 2018-01
**Status:** expert classification

## Finding

Munn et al. define a systematic review as "a robust, reproducible, structured critical synthesis of
existing research" and divide it into ten types by question: effectiveness, experiential
(qualitative), costs/economic evaluation, prevalence and/or incidence, diagnostic test accuracy,
etiology and/or risk, expert opinion/policy, psychometric, prognostic, and methodological. Each
type has its own question framework, for example PICO for effectiveness, CoCoPop for prevalence,
and PIRD for diagnostic accuracy. They exclude literature reviews, evidence maps, rapid,
integrative, scoping, and umbrella reviews from this typology.
