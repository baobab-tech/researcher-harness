# Evidence and Gap Maps Apply Systematic Review Methods to Display, Not Synthesise, the Evidence on a Question

**Topic:** Evidence and gap maps (EGMs) as an output type
**Source:** Howard White, Bianca Albers, Marie Gaarder, Hege Kornør, Julia Littell, Zack Marshall, Christine Mathew, Terri Pigott, Birte Snilstveit, Hugh Waddington, Vivian Welch (Campbell Collaboration), 2020
**Type:** peer-reviewed (methods guidance)
**URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8356343/
**DOI:** 10.1002/cl2.1125
**Published:** 2020-12
**Status:** organisational guidance

## Finding

An EGM is "a systematic evidence synthesis product which display[s] the available evidence relevant
to a specific research question," built with the same steps as a systematic review: a PICOS,
comprehensive search, screening against explicit criteria, and systematic coding. Its scope is
usually broader than a systematic review's. It is typically shown as a matrix, most often
interventions as rows and outcomes as columns, with studies in the cells. EGMs are used to identify
gaps for new research, collections of studies for review, and reviews that can feed guidelines.

## Key Data

| Metric | Value | Boundary / method |
|--------|-------|-------------------|
| Standard layout | intervention × outcome matrix | other axes permitted, e.g. intervention × region |
| Contents | systematic reviews and/or primary studies | sometimes other maps |
