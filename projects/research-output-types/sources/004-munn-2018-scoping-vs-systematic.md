# Scoping Reviews Map Evidence for Six Purposes; Systematic Reviews Answer a Question with Appraised Evidence

**Topic:** When to choose a scoping review over a systematic review
**Source:** Zachary Munn, Micah Peters, Cindy Stern, Catalin Tufanaru, Alexa McArthur, Edoardo Aromataris (JBI, University of Adelaide), 2018
**Type:** peer-reviewed
**URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6245623/
**DOI:** 10.1186/s12874-018-0611-x
**Published:** 2018-11
**Status:** methodological guidance

## Finding

Munn et al. list six purposes for a scoping review: to identify the types of available evidence in
a field; to clarify key concepts or definitions; to examine how research is conducted on a topic; to
identify key characteristics or factors related to a concept; as a precursor to a systematic
review; and to identify and analyse knowledge gaps. A systematic review is the choice when the
review must answer a question about the feasibility, appropriateness, meaningfulness, or
effectiveness of a practice. Scoping reviews generally do not assess risk of bias, so they do not
support practice recommendations.
