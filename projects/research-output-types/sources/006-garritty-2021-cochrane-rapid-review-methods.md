# Cochrane Defines a Rapid Review as a Systematic Review with Streamlined or Omitted Steps, and Issues 26 Method Recommendations

**Topic:** Rapid reviews: definition and permitted shortcuts
**Source:** Chantelle Garritty, Gerald Gartlehner, Barbara Nussbaumer-Streit, Valerie King, Candyce Hamel, Chris Kamel, Lisa Affengruber, Adrienne Stevens (Cochrane Rapid Reviews Methods Group), 2021
**Type:** peer-reviewed (methods guidance)
**URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7557165/
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.jclinepi.2020.10.007
**Published:** 2021-02
**Status:** consensus guidance

## Finding

The group defines a rapid review as "a form of knowledge synthesis that accelerates the process of
conducting a traditional systematic review through streamlining or omitting various methods to
produce evidence for stakeholders in a resource-efficient manner." It issues 26 interim
recommendations across stages: question setting with stakeholders, eligibility limits, searching,
screening, extraction, risk of bias, and synthesis. It recommends against single-reviewer
title/abstract screening, citing evidence that it misses studies, and recommends GRADE for
certainty of evidence. Systematic reviews typically take one to two years.

## Key Data

| Metric | Value | Boundary / method |
|--------|-------|-------------------|
| Recommendations | 26 | High or moderate agreement, or highest-scored option |
| Survey respondents | 63 of 119 (53%) | Representatives of 20 Cochrane entities |
| Rapid reviews analysed for the definition | 216, plus 90 methods articles | Prior scoping review |

## Methodology

Two scoping reviews, two primary methods studies, and a stakeholder survey with agreement
thresholds of 70% (high) and 50 to 69% (moderate).

## Limitations and conflicts

Interim guidance; several shortcuts lack evaluation. Authors are Cochrane methods group convenors.

## Relation to other sources

Matches the rapid family in Sutton et al. (`002`), which defines it by client negotiation over scope.
