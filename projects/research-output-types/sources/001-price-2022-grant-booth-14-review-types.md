# Grant and Booth's 2009 Typology Names 14 Review Types, Classified by Search, Appraisal, Synthesis, and Analysis

**Topic:** Review typologies; the 14-type list most widely cited outside health
**Source:** Carrie Price (Towson University), 2022, reviewing Maria J. Grant and Andrew Booth, 2009
**Type:** peer-reviewed (evidence summary of a methods paper)
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.18438/eblip30093
**DOI:** 10.18438/eblip30093 (Price 2022); 10.1111/j.1471-1842.2009.00848.x (Grant & Booth 2009, confirmed on Crossref)
**Published:** 2022-06
**Status:** description of a published typology

## Finding

Grant and Booth (2009) define 14 review types: critical review, literature review, mapping
review/systematic map, meta-analysis, mixed studies/mixed methods review, overview, qualitative
systematic review/qualitative evidence synthesis, rapid review, scoping review, state-of-the-art
review, systematic review, systematic search and review, systematized review, and umbrella review.
They classify types by method using the SALSA framework (Search, AppraisaL, Synthesis, Analysis),
after finding that review titles and self-descriptions did not classify reviews reliably.

## Methodology

Price summarises the 2009 paper and its reception. Grant and Booth ran a scoping search, drew on
their own experience, examined reviews from the *Health Information and Libraries Journal* review
feature and reviews from Ankem (2008), then characterised each type by its SALSA profile.

## Limitations and conflicts

The 2009 paper itself was not retrieved: Wiley and the Salford repository copy both served a
Cloudflare interstitial. The list of 14 comes from Price's summary, not from the original text.
Price is a health professions librarian; no funding is declared. The typology originates in health
and library science; Price reports uptake in social, environmental, and computer science, and the
humanities.

## Relation to other sources

Sutton et al. 2019 (`002`) extend this to 48 types and adopt its scoping/mapping distinction.
