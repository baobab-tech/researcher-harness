# Scoping Studies Serve Four Purposes and Follow Five Stages, with Optional Stakeholder Consultation

**Topic:** Scoping review method; the source framework
**Source:** Hilary Arksey, Lisa O'Malley (Social Policy Research Unit, University of York), 2005
**Type:** peer-reviewed
**URL:** https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/1618/1/Scopingstudies.pdf
**DOI:** 10.1080/1364557032000119616
**Published:** 2005-02
**Status:** methodological framework

## Finding

Arksey and O'Malley give four reasons for a scoping study: to examine the extent, range, and nature
of research activity; to determine the value of a full systematic review; to summarise and
disseminate findings; and to identify research gaps. The first two treat scoping as a step toward a
systematic review; the last two treat it as a method in its own right. The framework has five
stages (identify the question; identify relevant studies; select studies; chart the data; collate,
summarise and report) and an optional consultation exercise with stakeholders. Scoping does not
assess study quality, so it cannot find gaps where existing research is poor.

## Methodology

Framework derived from the authors' own scoping study of services for carers of people with mental
health problems.

## Limitations and conflicts

Based on a study funded by the UK NHS Service Delivery and Organisation programme. Single worked
example. Read from the White Rose repository copy.

## Relation to other sources

Munn et al. (`004`) extend the four purposes to six.
